Phishing Awareness Training

Learn to recognize phishing attacks and protect yourself online.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyber attack that uses deceptive messages or websites to trick people into revealing sensitive information or performing unsafe actions.

Attackers may pretend to be banks, companies, government organizations, friends, or other trusted individuals.

Why Learn About Phishing?

Understanding common phishing techniques can help you identify suspicious messages, links, and websites before they cause harm.

A few seconds of verification can prevent the loss of personal information, money, or access to an account.

Common Warning Signs

  • Urgent or threatening language
  • Suspicious or unfamiliar sender addresses
  • Unexpected links or attachments
  • Requests for passwords, OTPs, or sensitive information
  • Suspicious or misspelled website addresses
  • Offers that seem too good to be true

Types of Phishing

Email Phishing

Attackers send fraudulent emails designed to make the recipient click a malicious link, open an attachment, or provide sensitive information.

Spear Phishing

A targeted phishing attack designed for a specific person or organization. The attacker may use personal information to make the message appear legitimate.

Smishing

Phishing attacks carried out through SMS or text messages.

Vishing

Phishing attacks conducted through voice calls, where attackers may impersonate trusted organizations or authorities.

Clone Phishing

Attackers create a copy of a legitimate message or website and modify it to direct victims toward a malicious destination.

How to Identify Fake Websites

Phishing websites are designed to look similar to legitimate websites. Before entering sensitive information, check the following:

  • Check the website domain carefully.
  • Look for unusual spellings or extra characters in the domain.
  • Avoid clicking links received from suspicious messages.
  • Navigate to important services using their official website or application.
  • Do not assume that a website is trustworthy simply because it looks professional.

Social Engineering Tactics

Social engineering involves manipulating people into performing actions or revealing information that can benefit an attacker.

  • Urgency: Creating pressure so the victim acts quickly.
  • Fear: Threatening account suspension, financial loss, or other consequences.
  • Authority: Pretending to be a bank employee, government official, company representative, or other trusted authority.
  • Curiosity: Using interesting or unexpected information to encourage the victim to click.
  • Reward: Offering prizes, discounts, money, or other benefits.

Spot the Phishing Email

Examine the example below and identify the warning signs.

From: security-alert@bank-verification.example

Subject: URGENT: Your account will be suspended

Hello Customer,

We detected unusual activity on your account. Your account will be permanently suspended within 10 minutes unless you verify your information immediately.

Click the link below to prevent account suspension:

Verify Your Account Now

Thank you,
Security Department

What warning signs can you identify?

  • Urgent and threatening language
  • Pressure to act immediately
  • Unexpected request for account verification
  • Suspicious sender address
  • Link asking the user to take immediate action

How to Protect Yourself

  • Verify the sender before responding.
  • Avoid clicking unexpected links or attachments.
  • Check website addresses carefully.
  • Never share passwords, OTPs, or authentication codes.
  • Use multi-factor authentication whenever possible.
  • Keep your operating system and applications updated.
  • Report suspicious messages to the appropriate organization or platform.
